# AI Economic Financialization

> Financialization begins when economic exposure can be represented, valued, and transferred.

## From Measurement to Financialization

The previous research established a conceptual economic stack:

```text id="m7c4q1"
Economic Event
      ↓
Economic Activity
      ↓
Economic Output
      ↓
Economic Object
      ↓
Economic Measurement
      ↓
Economic Claim
      ↓
Valuation
      ↓
Financialization
```

The first layers describe economic reality.

The later layers describe how that reality can become economically and financially represented.

This distinction is important.

Mamiim does not begin with a financial instrument.

It begins with:

> **What happened?**

Then:

> **What was produced?**

Then:

> **What economic object exists?**

Only after these questions can be answered does financialization become meaningful.

---

## What Is Financialization?

Financialization can be understood broadly as the process through which an economic activity, asset, claim, or exposure becomes represented within a financial structure.

This may involve:

* ownership
* claims on future revenue
* claims on royalties
* exposure to an index
* participation in an economic outcome
* transferable economic rights
* programmable financial structures

The exact form depends on the underlying economic object and its legal and economic characteristics.

Therefore:

> **Financialization is not synonymous with tokenization.**

A token is one possible representation.

It is not the definition of financialization.

---

## The Four Layers

Mamiim separates four concepts:

```text id="j4p8s2"
Economic Object
      ↓
Economic Claim
      ↓
Valuation
      ↓
Financial Instrument
```

### Economic Object

Something persistent that exists economically.

### Economic Claim

An economic interest, potential right, or exposure associated with the object.

### Valuation

An assessment of what that object or claim may be worth.

### Financial Instrument

A marketable or programmable representation of economic exposure.

These layers should not be collapsed.

A claim may be:

* disputed

Therefore claim status matters.

## Contingent Claims

Some economic claims depend on future conditions.

For example:

```text id="a7n2m5"
Research Output
      ↓
Qualifying Condition
      ↓
Potential Prize
```

The economic exposure exists as a possibility.

But the payment has not occurred.

Therefore:

> **A contingent claim is not realized revenue.**

This distinction is essential for AI-native economic objects because many valuable outputs may have uncertain future outcomes.

## Valuation

Once an economic object or claim has been sufficiently identified, someone may attempt to value it.

Valuation can use different approaches.

For example:

```text id="w5p8q3"
Historical Revenue
Comparable Assets
Expected Cash Flow
Usage
Network Activity
Market Demand
Replacement Cost
Probability-Weighted Outcomes
```

Different objects may require different valuation models.

There is no universal valuation method for all AI economic objects.

---

## Measurement ≠ Valuation

Mamiim therefore maintains:

```text id="c9x4m7"
Measurement
"What happened?"
```

versus:

```text id="v3k8p2"
Valuation
"What might it be worth?"
```

Measurement can be evidence-based.

Valuation necessarily introduces assumptions about future economic significance.

For example:

```text id="m2q6r8"
10,000 completed tasks
```

is a measurement.

While:

```text id="f8n3w5"
This agent is worth €2M
```

is a valuation.

The second cannot simply be derived from the first without a model.

---

## Price ≠ Economic History

Market price provides another layer.

```text id="p7m4x9"
Economic History
      ↓
Valuation
      ↓
Market
      ↓
Price
```

Price represents what market participants are willing to pay at a particular moment.

It is not a complete record of how the economic object came into existence.

Therefore:

> **Price is market opinion. Economic history is evidence.**

Mamiim is primarily concerned with preserving the latter.

---

## Tokenization Is One Mechanism

In crypto-native systems, financialization is often associated with tokenization.

But:

```text id="n4p7s1"
Financialization
        ≠
Tokenization
```

Tokenization can make an economic representation:

* programmable
* transferable
* composable
* machine-readable

But a token without a meaningful underlying economic object or claim does not automatically create economic value.

Therefore:

> **The token is the representation. The economic object is the underlying reference.**

* what evidence supports it

This creates a dependency:

```text id="j6p2v8"

        ↓
Financial Legibility
        ↓
```

Mamiim operates primarily in the first layer.

## Event Contract ≠ Economic Object

This distinction is particularly important.

An Economic Object answers:

> What economically meaningful thing exists?

> **What exposure can someone take to a defined future outcome?**

For example:

```text id="v8m2q6"

      ↓

      ↓
Event Contract
```

The research output remains the underlying economic object.

The event contract is a financial representation of uncertainty around a future outcome.

## What Mamiim Should Not Assume

### Every economic object should be tokenized.

Tokenization is only one possible representation.

### Every measurable object has a financial claim.

Measurement does not create ownership.

### Every claim has a market.

Economic interest and market demand are different.

### Every valuation is objective.

Valuation contains assumptions.

### Every financial product reflects the underlying economy perfectly.

Financial instruments introduce their own structures and risks.

### Onchain representation creates legal ownership.

Programmability does not automatically establish legal rights.

### Financialization should happen immediately.

Economic objects may need time to mature.
